WHERE LEADS LEAK

Fix the handoffs that leave electrical enquiries waiting.

The objective is not generic automation. It is to make specific handoffs around missed calls, after-hours enquiries, approved routing, callback or booking and follow-up easier to handle consistently.

Missed-call recovery

Give callers a clear next path after a missed call instead of leaving the enquiry in voicemail or an unowned inbox.

After-hours intake

Capture caller-provided contact and enquiry details outside normal coverage and route them into the approved workflow.

Structured detail capture

Pass caller-provided job context to the responsible person without repeated manual copying between systems.

Service-area and urgency routing

Move an enquiry to the appropriate queue, coverage area or responsible team member while keeping the next owner visible.

Human callback and booking handoffs

Connect the agreed next step to a callback, site visit or booking process with clear human ownership.

Follow-up and CRM sync

Keep lead records, statuses and useful context aligned across the tools your team already uses.

ELECTRICAL LEAD RECOVERY FAQ

What this engagement actually means.

Does this assess an electrical hazard or site safety?

No. The system can capture caller-provided details and route or escalate the enquiry, but qualified people and emergency services remain responsible for safety decisions and emergency judgement.

Can this handle after-hours electrical enquiries?

Those are common starting points. The appropriate acknowledgement, intake, routing and escalation rules depend on the systems, coverage and operating handoffs your team already uses.

Is this a generic chatbot?

No. Conversational tools can be one component, but the core work is the lead path: event intake, context, routing, human callback or booking handoff, follow-up state and operational visibility.

Where do technical tools fit?

CRM, telephony, API and workflow tools are implementation choices. They support the lead-recovery process rather than defining the service category.

What should an electrical contractor address first?

Start with a measurable bottleneck: a missed-call path, after-hours intake, unclear service-area handoff or follow-up with no owner. The first build should be narrow enough to validate in the real operation.
