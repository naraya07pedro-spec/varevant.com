WHERE LEADS LEAK

Fix the handoffs that leave plumbing enquiries waiting.

The objective is not generic automation. It is to make specific handoffs around missed calls, after-hours enquiries, quote follow-up and booking easier to handle consistently.

Missed-call text-back

Trigger a timely acknowledgement after a missed call and give the enquiry a clear next path instead of leaving it in voicemail.

After-hours enquiry response

Capture context from calls, forms or messages outside normal coverage and route it into the appropriate response workflow.

Web-lead routing

Move form context to the right person or queue without repeated manual copying between inboxes and systems.

Quote and follow-up recovery

Use status-aware follow-up so open enquiries and quotes have a visible next step rather than relying on memory alone.

Callback and booking handoffs

Connect agreed next steps to a callback, booking request, calendar or internal queue while keeping ownership clear.

CRM and operational sync

Keep lead records, statuses and important context aligned across the tools your team already uses.

PLUMBING LEAD RECOVERY FAQ

What this engagement actually means.

Do you replace our plumbing CRM?

Not by default. The first question is whether your current system can remain the source of truth while the lead-response handoffs around it become more consistent.

Can this handle missed calls and web enquiries?

Those are common starting points. The appropriate response, routing and follow-up rules depend on the systems and operating handoffs your team already uses.

Is this a generic chatbot?

No. Conversational tools can be one component, but the core work is the lead path: event intake, context, routing, callback or booking handoff, follow-up state and operational visibility.

Where do technical tools fit?

CRM, telephony, API and workflow tools are implementation choices. They support the lead-recovery process rather than defining the service category.

What should a plumbing business address first?

Start with a measurable bottleneck: a missed-call path, slow web-lead response, an unclear callback handoff or follow-up that has no owner. The first build should be narrow enough to validate in the real operation.
